<p class="lead">
  Keeping up with AI in 2026 is a part-time job, and most engineers are
  doing it badly — myself included, for a year. Here's the system I
  landed on that takes ~90 minutes a week and beats every "must-read"
  newsletter I've tried.
</p>

## /01 The problem isn't volume, it's recency-bias {#problem}

The firehose feels new every day, but 80% of what crosses my feed in any
given week is either (a) a benchmark that won't generalize, (b) a thread
about a paper I'll never implement, or (c) a model release I won't have
time to evaluate for at least a month. Recency-bias eats the day.

The fix is to read on a delay.

## /02 The 90-minute weekly batch {#batch}

Every Sunday I spend 90 minutes on a single tab and nothing else. Three
blocks, in this order:

1. **Releases (15 min)** — what shipped this week, from the model labs and from the tools we use.
2. **Signal sources (45 min)** — a fixed list of ~9 people whose RSS I trust.
3. **Working notes (30 min)** — turn the above into a one-page diff against last week's notes.

Nothing else gets read during the week. If something is genuinely
urgent, it'll cross my path three times by Sunday. That's the filter.

## /03 What's on the signal list {#list}

I rotate this list quarterly. Current cut:

- The model lab changelogs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google AI, Mistral).
- Two researchers per major capability area I care about.
- One contrarian who I disagree with productively.
- Hacker News only via [hckrnews.com](https://hckrnews.com) (top 10/day).
- The Simbian internal "ai-news" channel, batched.

I dropped Twitter as a primary source in 2025. It's still good for
real-time vibes; it's terrible for compounding understanding.

## /04 The one-page weekly diff {#diff}

The output of the batch is a single markdown file. Six headings, never
more. Stays under one page. The discipline of fitting it on one page
forces ranking.

```markdown
# Week of 04 May 2026

## New capabilities worth trying
## Things that broke (us or others)
## Papers I'd implement if I had time
## What I changed my mind about
## What I'm watching next week
## (one) link worth your time
```

This file ships to the team Slack on Monday at 9am.

## /05 Why this works {#why}

Three reasons, in order of importance:

- **Decision-relevance over novelty.** The batch is filtered through
  "would this change what I build this week?" Most things don't.
- **Asynchronous from launches.** I read at my pace, not the labs'.
- **Compounds across weeks.** The diff is a diff — week N references
  week N-1. After three months it's the most useful doc in my notes.

If you take one thing: read on a delay. The half-life of an AI take is
about four days. Most of them are wrong, and you'll know which by
Sunday.
